Combat & equipment reference

Stats & Equipment.

Pixel does not treat every combat effect, enchant proc and registry key as the same kind of statistic. This reference starts with the core stats whose producer-to-consumer path is established, then shows where equipment-family rules and layered mechanics take over.

01 / Core stats

A stat is documented because something actually consumes it.

The table intentionally stays smaller than the underlying registry. A declared key is not enough: the current guide requires a demonstrated gameplay path before presenting a stat as part of the active core.

Damage-per-hit and attack cadence are separate.

A melee weapon cannot be evaluated from Attack Damage alone. Attack Speed changes how often that damage can be delivered, so sustained output depends on both values together.

02 / Survivability

Defense and Max Health form one curve.

Defense is not converted into a flat percentage in isolation. Pixel's mitigation model scales it against the defender's maximum health and applies a hard ceiling.

Source formula—

Maximum reduction—The ceiling prevents Defense from turning continued stat growth into complete damage immunity.

Max Health still matters after mitigation because it enlarges the health pool the remaining damage must consume.

Mitigation belongs once in the damage path.

Player-facing documentation treats mitigation as one target-side calculation. Enchants or other modifiers should not create duplicate damage events merely to obtain a second reduction pass.

03 / Equipment families

Shared stats do not erase equipment identity.

Two families can use the same underlying stat while behaving differently through cadence, slot, enchant pool or a dedicated mechanic.

04 / Layered mechanics

Not every real mechanic needs to become another base stat.

Penetration, Lifesteal, boss-conditional effects and area damage are genuine gameplay behaviors, but their owning systems define when and how they apply.

Ownership prevents semantic drift.

For example, Lifesteal belongs to the system that grants and evaluates it; it is not interchangeable with Max Health. Penetration modifies mitigation semantics; it is not simply another Attack Damage field. Detailed effect behavior remains in the Enchantments and Talisman guides.

05 / Scope boundary

Unknown coverage stays visibly unknown.

Pixel Guides does not turn a registry declaration or an old planning spreadsheet into proof of current gameplay behavior. These areas stay outside the active table until their consumer path is re-verified.

Historical stat inventories are discovery aids, not current contracts.

Older spreadsheets and the legacy GitBook remain useful for identifying candidate stats and intended slots, but a green marker from an old planning document does not override the current producer-to-consumer audit.

06 / Documentation confidence

Core meaning is source verified; deployment-sensitive fixes remain qualified.

This guide deliberately avoids converting a recent source-ready combat correction into a production claim. It documents stable stat semantics and flags the areas that still need their own evidence pass.
